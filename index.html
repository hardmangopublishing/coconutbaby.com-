Walk the baby aisle and coconut is on half the labels. It is in the oil, the balm, the diaper cream, the shampoo, the mattress, the play mat, the yogurt cup, the teething biscuit. Some of that is real — coconut oil is a genuinely good emollient and coir is a genuinely good mattress fill. Some of it is a picture of a coconut on a bottle that is mostly water and fragrance.

This site sorts the two. Every guide here opens the same way: a short panel saying who the product is for and — the part most shopping sites leave out — who should skip it entirely. If a product does not deserve an honest skip line, it does not belong on a page here.

None of this is medical advice, and none of it replaces your pediatrician. Baby skin, baby sleep, and baby food are three areas where a confident stranger on the internet can do real harm. Where a question belongs to a doctor, these guides say so and stop.

How this site works

Four guides, no newsletter wall, no ranked lists

There are no numbered top-ten lists here, because a top-ten list is a format that has to be filled whether or not ten things deserve the slot. Each guide instead explains what to look for on the label, and then points at Amazon searches for that category so you can compare current prices, sizes and reviews yourself. Products change formulas, sellers go out of stock, and a hardcoded pick goes stale within a season. A description of what a good one looks like does not.

Where a claim is a matter of pediatric guidance rather than shopping — safe sleep, allergy introduction, anything going in a baby's mouth — the guides state the standard advice at a general level and tell you to confirm it with your own pediatrician, because that guidance is updated periodically and your baby's situation is not general.
